# Literature triage — `papers/litreview` (9 drafts)

| Paper | Topic | Question | Method | Data | Main finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fritsche-2021 | Fiscal | How do government spending multipliers vary with macroeconomic uncertainty? | Markov-switching model exploiting time-varying volatility to identify spending shocks | US macroeconomic time series | Average multiplier persistently above one (crowds in consumption and investment), but significantly smaller when volatility is high |
| sheremirov-2019 | Fiscal | Effects of government spending on output across advanced and developing countries? | Military spending as exogenous instrument for spending shocks | Novel military-spending data, 129 countries, 1988–2013 | One-year multipliers of 0.75–0.85; larger in recessions, closed economies, and under fixed exchange rates |
| vangemert-2022 | Fiscal | Do fiscal multipliers differ across categories of government spending? | Local projections of cumulative multipliers, state-level defense vs. non-defense shocks | US state-level federal defense and non-defense spending | Defense spending yields lower multipliers than non-defense, so defense-only studies underestimate the overall multiplier |
| azar-2019 | Labour | Why are minimum-wage employment effects often near zero — does monopsony explain it? | Fine occupation-level spatial variation in labor-market concentration | US labor-market concentration data, low-wage retail sector | Employment effects become less negative (even positive) as concentration rises, supporting the monopsony explanation |
| bossler-2019 | Labour | Employment effects of Germany's 2015 statutory minimum wage? | Difference-in-differences on establishment exposure to the minimum wage | IAB Establishment Panel (German establishment microdata) | Wages rose 3.8–6.3%, employment fell ~1.7% in affected establishments (elasticity −0.2 to −0.4), mainly via fewer hires |
| caliendo-2018 | Labour | Short-run employment effects of Germany's 2015 minimum-wage reform? | Regional variation in minimum-wage "bite" across 141 regional labor markets | Structure of Earnings Survey 2014, administrative regional employment, SOEP | No pronounced effect on regular employment (≤~78k jobs, ~0.3%), but mini-jobs dropped ~180k (~2.4%) |
| guler-2021 | Monetary | Does policy credibility help anchor inflation expectations in IT emerging economies? | Empirical panel/time-series tests of credibility's effect on expectations | Six emerging IT economies (Turkey, Brazil, Czech Rep., Chile, Poland, South Africa) | Credibility significantly reduces inflation expectations and strengthens the target as an anchor |
| mlangeni-2023 | Monetary | How does monetary policy affect inflation expectations across heterogeneous agents in South Africa? | Vector Error Correction (VEC) model and Granger causality tests | South African financial, business, trade-union sector expectations, Q1 2000–Q4 2022 | Effects differ by sector; repo-rate changes Granger-cause expectations in business and trade-union sectors |
| rehman-2024 | Monetary | How does policy credibility affect inflation expectations in Asian economies? | Wavelet-based quantile regression with a credibility index as instrument | Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, India, Pakistan, 1990q1–2021q4 | Credibility significantly affects expectations in Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Pakistan; differs in India — argues for country-tailored policy |

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Fiscal** cluster converges on multiplier *heterogeneity* — the size depends on the state of the cycle/uncertainty (fritsche), country structure (sheremirov), and spending category (vangemert).
- **Labour** cluster is dominated by two German 2015 minimum-wage studies (bossler, caliendo) that reach broadly compatible "small/modest" employment effects, plus azar's US monopsony mechanism that rationalizes why such effects are muted.
- **Monetary** cluster is a tight theme: central-bank *credibility* and inflation-expectation anchoring in emerging/EM-Asian economies (guler, rehman empirical-credibility; mlangeni sectoral heterogeneity).
